# Design

Help the team express its idea through an agreed visual or sensory direction, then apply it to the prototype. Work with apps, games, installations, art, or participatory experiences. Do not replace the tested behavior with a different concept or assume every medium is a website.

## Read the project and prototype

Read `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` in the active project root, creating it if missing. Recover the project Page and prototype locations, read fresh Page content, and inspect the actual prototype or supplied mockup. Ask for their locations only if still missing. Preserve participant edits and the intended audience, context, message, and behavior.

For apps, websites, and webapps, carry forward the brief's agreed Primary user flows, competitor findings, and existing Mobbin references. Use Mobbin's `redesign-screen` skill to design the actual greybox or wireframes, one screen, section, or component at a time. Its focused design searches do not restart Start's competitor research or flow recommendation. Resolve any material change to agreed flows with the team and update the brief.

Check whether the team has tried the greybox and is ready to work on aesthetics. If that is unclear, ask one focused question. If they explicitly choose to design before a behavior review, explain that behavior remains unresolved and honor their choice; do not invent a completed review.

## Ask for their references

Open the aesthetic conversation by asking whether they have a mood board, references, or a direction in mind. Accept images, links, physical examples, descriptive words, and references from outside the medium. Inspect supplied material rather than inferring it from filenames.

Reflect the qualities they seem to value and let them correct the reading. Consider typography, color, composition, imagery, material, light, sound, motion, or spatial feel as relevant. Ask about unclear priorities instead of applying a generic style.

For apps, websites, and webapps, choosing Design includes the focused Mobbin research required by the redesign workflow below; do not ask for a separate search approval. Reuse the team's references and answered questions. For other media or additional mood-board research, offer a focused search and wait for their answer unless already authorized. Use appropriate project, art, spatial, game, material, or sensory references. Cite actual source links and distinguish inspiration from participant decisions.

## Redesign digital greyboxes or wireframes with Mobbin

For apps, websites, and webapps, reuse the installed Mobbin skills and connection, or follow the installation guidance in [Mobbin research](../start/references/mobbin-research.md#ensure-mobbin-is-available). Read the installed `best-practices` and `redesign-screen` skills and the supporting references they route to. There is no separate redesign MCP tool: the skill guides reference research and actual design work through the tools available for the source.

Inspect the real greybox or wireframe first. Pass its code, captures, or design-file source, the relevant agreed flow, team goals, mood references, existing Mobbin links, and constraints into the redesign. Start with a representative screen or section in the primary journey. Scope each pass to a concrete target; do not ask Mobbin to redesign the whole product at once. Reuse known decisions and ask only about material gaps, keeping one main question per turn.

Use `--explore` when the team needs concrete directions to compare; keep variations as samples until they select one. Use `--targeted` for agreed changes or a specific problem. Let `redesign-screen` invoke Mobbin's `search` skill for the references it needs; do not run a duplicate preliminary screen search. Preserve working behavior, content, routes, and agreed scope. A greybox's placeholder styling is provisional: establish and confirm the team's own aesthetic direction through the sample checkpoint below, and supply that direction as the product's design system for the redesign. A theme change is an explicit design choice, separate from borrowing an interface structure.

Produce the output in the source's supported form: prototype edits, editable design frames when tools can write them, or clearly labeled image mockups. Show real before/after captures or variations where possible and briefly explain the observed patterns behind the changes with actual source links. After the team selects the sample, apply its direction to the remaining agreed screens in bounded passes and verify the core flow still works.

If Mobbin access fails, follow its failure guidance and report the actual blocker. Do not claim a Mobbin-backed redesign or replace its evidence with memory. Preserve the prototype and record this step as pending; clearly distinguish any separate design work the team chooses to continue.

## Refine the direction

Discuss references and show concrete samples of the same screen, scene, object, or moment so comparisons are meaningful. For an undecided team, offer up to three distinct directions with brief explanations. If they already have a clear direction, start with one sample and refine it. Three alternatives are not mandatory.

Make each sample respond to the project's audience, context, and message, not just a fashionable style. Show images or interactive previews when available; do not describe an uncreated mood board as an existing artifact.

Iterate with the team on the sample. A reference preference does not itself confirm a full design direction. Wait for an explicit selection or instruction to apply the agreed direction before making broad changes to the prototype. Do not interpret silence as approval.

## Apply and verify

Apply the chosen direction consistently to the agreed prototype. Keep its core behavior and scope intact. If an aesthetic choice changes comprehension, timing, participation, or interaction, explain the tradeoff and resolve that choice with the team.

Subagents can create bounded assets or implement independent visual areas where useful; one lead agent integrates the work and maintains a coherent direction. Avoid unnecessary delegation or simultaneous changes to the same files.

Check the actual result: the important interaction still works, text is legible in its context, controls remain usable, and visual or sensory elements support the intended experience. Present the result and let the team refine it. Report any aspect that cannot be assessed without a physical setup or participant trial.

## Record and finish

Update the same project Page with the source references, selected samples where embedding is supported, agreed direction, implemented choices, and unresolved matters. Maintain `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` as these decisions happen, recording actual reference sources, AI tools/models and their uses, asset attribution, experiments, and results. Distinguish a suggested direction from an applied design and preserve current participant edits. Read current content and Page guidance before edits, use fresh guards, upload images through supported native file tools, and verify saved content. Keep ordinary text editable and retain source attribution separately from image references. If Pages is unavailable, record pending Page changes in the knowledge base and report the unsaved shared-document step.

Provide the actual prototype link or location and the Page link. Update Workshop progress to describe what is complete and what still needs a check. Do not automatically generate a demo video, slides, or a new scope of work. Publishing or changing sharing requires the relevant authorization.
