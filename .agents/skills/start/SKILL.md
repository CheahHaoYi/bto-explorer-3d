# Start

Help the participant express their idea and finish with a concise PRD in one project Page. Support apps, games, installations, artworks, and participatory experiences. Aim for about 30 minutes; building and prototype testing belong to Develop.

Ask one main question per turn. Listen for answers to later questions and reuse them. Probe meaningful gaps, not every possible field. Keep assumptions visibly provisional. If resuming an existing project, read its Page and continue from the recorded checkpoint rather than asking the opening question again.

Read and maintain `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` in the active project root alongside the shared Page. Recover existing links and decisions from it, reconcile fresh Page edits, and update it after meaningful answers and checkpoints. Capture idea, persona assumptions, context, references, chosen approach, scope, open questions, and tools/resources actually used. Create the file if absent. Keep a concise summary; do not prefill a fictional participant project or ask submission-only team questions during this conversation.

## 1. What

For a fresh session, ask exactly: **What are you hoping to make?**

Wait for the participant's answer. Reflect the idea briefly and clarify only what you need to understand it. Do not open with logistics, a questionnaire, a build plan, or suggested projects.

## 2. Who and why

Ask: **Who is this for?**

Draw out a provisional persona through follow-ups grounded in the answer. Explore relevant demographics or life circumstances; needs; existing behaviors; pain points; and values. For example: “What do they do now?” or “What matters most to them in that situation?” Do not infer motivations from demographics or invent survey findings.

Surface why through this conversation: why would this person care, use it, attend, or participate? What would they hope to feel, understand, express, or accomplish? Keep audience goals distinct from the team's artistic message. Both can matter.

Use participant or audience language where user language does not fit. Briefly note other roles only when they materially affect the experience, such as an installation facilitator or people whose stories are represented. Do not turn this into a separate stakeholder exercise.

## 3. When and where

Ask when they envision someone experiencing it, then clarify where. Use concrete examples appropriate to the idea: day or night, indoors or outdoors, alone or with others, passing by or intentionally visiting.

Capture enough context to make the situation understandable. Do not require a formal scenario exercise or ask how to test it at this stage. Existing answers may already supply the context.

## 4. Pyramid and reflection

Create the project Page now if one has not been selected. Discover the active Pages tools and follow their current schema and applicable guidance. Use one Page throughout this project; if no destination has been selected, create a private Page without a parent. Record its real returned link.

Write the fuller reflection using the Project idea, Audience and motivations, and Context sections in [the PRD outline](references/prd-outline.md). Include a short provisional audience profile, grounded only in their answers. Mark unanswered items as open.

Generate a clear strategic pyramid image from those answers: what, who, and why at the foundation; when and where in the middle; how at the top labeled “To explore.” Use short, readable labels. Do not invent the how or substitute demographic stereotypes. Use an available image tool or create a rendered diagram image. Check the image against the answers, upload it through supported Page image tools, and insert its returned native image reference. Keep the fuller explanation editable outside the image. Verify the save and image rendering when preview is available.

If image generation or embedding is unavailable, keep the text reflection in the Page, explain the specific missing image step, and offer to proceed with the review. Do not silently substitute an unrelated picture or claim the image is embedded.

Reflect the main meaning in chat and ask whether this captures their idea. Let them correct it and update the Page. Wait for explicit confirmation of this framing before researching.

## 5. Explore related experiences

Tell them briefly what kind of references you will look for. Search a small set of real, relevant precedents, usually three. Route by medium:

- Apps, websites, and webapps: after the idea is explicitly confirmed in step 4, follow [Mobbin competitor and primary-flow research](references/mobbin-research.md), including its connection-selection rules before searching. Use the participant's selected Mobbin connector; otherwise prefer the connected Mobbin app over a standalone MCP connection. Reuse available official skills and tools before installing anything. Use Mobbin's `search` skill for related competitors alongside App Store or other relevant store listings, official websites, and web research. Then use that skill with `search_flows` to lay out the recommended primary user flows for team review.
- Games: real games, official storefront or developer pages, gameplay material, and interaction references. Do not claim to access a personal Steam library without a tool and permission.
- Installations, art, and creative technology: artists' projects, exhibitions, demonstrations, and documented creative experiments. Consult libraries or framework examples when they reveal a relevant possibility; a technology API alone is not an experience precedent.
- Participatory or community projects: documented activities, formats, rituals, or services relevant to the idea.

Use actual tools and report limitations accurately. Prefer first-party project sources. Cite each reference with a direct link and show a preview when supported. Explain the relevant aspect, what it might suggest, and any important difference. Label an analogy as an analogy; do not manufacture a close match.

Add these references to the same Page. For apps, websites, and webapps, present the competitor findings followed by the recommended primary user flows and ask whether the team wants to use or change the recommendation; their answer can also identify the reference aspects they value. For other media, ask which aspects, if any, feel like the direction they want to explore. They can combine aspects, reject the selection, or propose different references. Do not interpret liking a reference as approval of flows or permission to copy its full design. Keep additional searching focused. Once the team explicitly agrees to the flows, add them to the brief and knowledge base as confirmed.

## 6. How

Choose the question that fits:

- A product or service: “What is the main difference between your idea and these examples?”
- A game or playful experience: “What should feel distinctive about yours?”
- Art or culture: “What belief or message do you want to express, and how do you want people to encounter it?”

If uniqueness is unimportant, explore the intended approach or expression instead. Their chosen reference can also be a legitimate starting point. Do not impose a business differentiator, innovation claim, or technical stack.

Capture their answer, refine ambiguity conversationally, and update the how in the Page and pyramid image if its content changed. Keep their intended meaning separate from agent suggestions. If the chosen approach or subsequent scope changes materially alter an agreed primary flow, revise that flow and confirm the change before recording it as agreed.

## 7. Scope only when needed

If the approach appears feasible for a one-day hackathon, record it without a prioritization exercise. Ask about time, team, available materials or equipment only where needed to understand feasibility; reuse known event constraints.

If they propose several substantial directions, have unclear scope, or exceed available resources, explain the concrete scope issue in ordinary language. Suggest what to keep and ask for their judgment. Use Must, Should, Could, and Won't today when helpful. Prioritize features for software or experience elements for other media. Do not make every project use MoSCoW.

Do not add a separate “what experience do you want to create,” “what must work today,” or testing questionnaire. Derive the brief from the answers already given, asking only to resolve material gaps.

## 8. PRD and handoff

Complete the same Page using [the PRD outline](references/prd-outline.md), preserving participant edits. It is the project requirements document even when the project is not software. Keep it useful and short; omit irrelevant fields and empty framework sections. Include agreed scope, relevant constraints, open assumptions, and a suggested demonstration derived from their intended experience. Label that demonstration as a suggestion until accepted; do not add acceptance criteria they never agreed to.

Record Start as “Ready for team review.” Read back the saved content. Provide the real Page link and invite them to correct the brief there or in chat. Their approval of the earlier pyramid is not approval of a newly generated PRD. They may invoke Develop with the corrected Page as their instruction to move into building.

Suggested handoff: “Your project brief is here: [Page link]. Edit anything you want to change. When you're ready, use Develop with this Page.” Do not begin implementation or aesthetic exploration automatically.

Pages is the shared PRD destination; the local knowledge base also preserves project context. If Pages cannot save, say so, record the pending update in `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md`, and keep the Page handoff incomplete. Do not claim the local file is a saved Page. Update Workshop progress and the knowledge base with the current checkpoint and unresolved matters so the conversation can resume.
