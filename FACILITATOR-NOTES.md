# Facilitator notes

This draft captures Lucas's brain dump on 9 October 2026 and the submission and knowledge-base additions requested on 10 October 2026. The participant walkthrough has not been run. No workbook or example fitness project has been created.

## Decisions captured

- Four skills: Start, Develop, Design, Submit. Plan and Polish can act as message aliases.
- Start opens only with “What are you hoping to make?”
- Explore who through a proto-persona conversation: relevant demographics, needs, behaviors, pain points, and values. Draw why from their motivations and desired outcomes.
- Explore when and where, including time of day and indoors or outdoors where relevant.
- Generate a strategic pyramid image and a fuller editable explanation in Pages, then reflect it back for correction and confirmation.
- Research related experiences after that confirmation. Route research by medium and show concrete references before discussing what makes the idea distinctive or expressive.
- Ask how in the project's own terms: difference, uniqueness, belief, message, or approach. Differentiation is not obligatory.
- Use prioritization only when participants need help narrowing scope. Suggest a cut, hear their judgment, then record the agreed scope.
- Finish Start with a short PRD in the same Page. The team can correct it before invoking Develop.
- Develop reads that corrected PRD, creates a plan and greybox, and supports trying and refining the experience.
- Design asks for a mood board and can offer reference searches. Refine the direction together and then apply it.
- Pages holds the shared project documents. The 10 October update also requires a local `PROJECT-KNOWLEDGE.md` maintained by all four skills; it preserves context without claiming to replace an unsaved Page.
- Submit follows the supplied Common Ground seven-section template, drafting from context and asking only for missing facts. It enforces the two whole-section 100-word caps, up to two tracks, conditional reflections, real project links, collaboration, and actual AI/resource attribution. It does not send an external submission.
- No Demo skill in this version. No automatic video, slides, or workbook generation.

## Framework adaptation

Brian L.M. Boyl's strategic pyramid places what, who, and why at its foundation, when and where above them, and how at the top. In this workshop, how remains open in the initial pyramid. Research then informs an answer about the team's intended approach, distinctive quality, or expression. Update the same pyramid when that answer is agreed.

This is a workshop adaptation, not a claim that Boyl defines how solely as differentiation. Reference: [Interaction for Designers](https://www.routledge.com/Interaction-for-Designers-How-To-Make-Things-People-Love/Boyl/p/book/9780415787253).

## Timing to test

Start has a 30-minute target. A tentative allocation is 3 minutes for the idea, 8 for audience and why, 5 for context and reflection, 6 for precedents, and 8 for approach, scope, and PRD. These are pacing hints, not timed participant gates. Image generation and tool availability may affect pacing.

## First tester notes

Use a workout tracker or another fitness idea for the first walkthrough if that is still interesting. Leave these observations for the actual walkthrough:

- Did the audience probes deepen the idea or feel repetitive?
- Was why captured naturally?
- Did the pyramid reflect the intended idea without inventing a how?
- Did the references open possibilities or steer the idea too strongly?
- Did conditional prioritization appear only when needed?
- Was the PRD concise, accurate, and easy to edit?
- Did Develop preserve Page edits and build the appropriate kind of prototype?
- Did Design help express the idea rather than apply a generic style?
- Did the knowledge base preserve decisions, actual build evidence, and attribution between stages?
- Did Submit recover known answers, ask only for missing facts, and keep the two whole-section descriptions within 100 words?
